Character Episode Template

How to take a character sheet and come out with a finished episode. Seven episodes, three characters and four visual registers were produced through this path; every number below was measured on the workstation, not estimated. The pictures are ready to be a house style. The audio is not, and the last section says so plainly.

A rule of thumb that has held across all five: one drawing per spoken sentence, four to six seconds on screen, thirty-six seconds of GPU per drawing. A two-minute episode is a lunch break; five minutes is an afternoon.

The path

01

Turn the character sheet into a master prompt

Read the sheet as a specification and write it out as one paragraph in this order: who they are → the head → the face → the body and limbs → the one identifying accessory → the drawing medium → the background → what must not be in frame. Name real colours where the sheet gives them. End with a plain, evenly filled background and an explicit ban on lettering.

Generate it with the text-to-image graph at the channel's aspect ratio, at a fixed seed, and keep that seed. Everything downstream is an edit of this one picture.

02

Write the script as beats, not paragraphs

One sentence of narration is one beat is one drawing. Structure the episode in five parts — hook, setup, cause and effect, the turn, the close — and put the turn at roughly four fifths of the running time. Write the spoken line first and the picture second: the picture illustrates the sentence, never the other way round.

03

Build every shot prompt from four fixed parts

[IDENTITY]  Use the attached image as the authoritative character identity
            reference. Keep the exact same character: <the four or five
            features that must never change>. Never redraw him as <the
            thing the model keeps drifting toward>.

[BEAT]      <what is in this frame: who, doing what, where, in what light>

[WORLD]     <the medium, the palette, the background, the light source>
            No text, no letters, no numbers. <Aspect> composition with
            room around him.

[NEGATIVE]  text, letters, numbers, watermark, + the failure modes of this
            particular character

IDENTITY, WORLD and NEGATIVE are constants for the whole episode. Only BEAT changes. This is what makes fifty-four shots look like one film.

04

Generate, review one at a time, send back what fails

Look at every frame before it goes in the cut. Across five episodes the first-pass keep rate was roughly seven in nine. Rejections are cheap — a still is under a minute — and the two things worth rejecting for are readable text and a shot that quietly drew the wrong place.

05

Cut, then narrate, then check the numbers

Build the timeline from approved takes, render, and read the meter: the delivery target is −16 LUFS integrated with true peak under −1.5 dBTP. Every episode above landed within 0.2 LU of target without hand adjustment.

Rules, and what each one cost to learn

R1 · one world

State the background in every prompt, identically

Letting each shot describe its own background produced sixteen frames in grey, cream and white by turns. It is invisible in a contact sheet and reads as a flicker at every cut. One sentence — the exact ground colour, evenly filled, plus the light source — fixed all sixteen at once.

R2 · attach, never describe

The master is attached to every shot, through an edit model

Measured on the same three poses: the cheaper edit graph kept the head, the stroke and the colour and lost the proportions, the scale, and one pose's legs. Qwen-Image-Edit kept all of them. Describing a character in words instead of attaching a picture does not hold past the third frame.

R3 · a diagram is not an edit

A shot with no character in it must not carry the reference

Two diagram beats came back with the character standing in front of the waves, twice, past a prompt saying "a diagram with no people in it at all" and a negative naming person, figure and face. An attached identity reference is not a suggestion. Character-free beats are text-to-image shots with no reference attached.

R4 · no generated words

Every word on screen is composited afterwards

Four episodes, the same failure: newsprint reading NEAYII STR, a headline reading erdsys KEPT vepist alejr. Negative prompts naming legible text, headlines, mastheads and lettering do not stop it — there is no negative prompt for "spell correctly". Remove the surface from the frame, and put the words in at the edit.

R5 · stills, not clips

Motion comes from the edit, not from a video model

Image-to-video redrew a clean still and wrote a headline on it; abandoned its start frame entirely on another shot; and returned three consecutive opening clips at 0.33 mean luma change with every sample near-static. A stick figure animated this way loses its limbs. Push-ins and pans in the editor do the work at no risk.

R6 · the master sets the shape

Author the master at the channel's aspect ratio

The edit graph takes its output size from the reference image, not from the project canvas. A square master makes square shots however the project is configured. A landscape channel needs a landscape master.

R7 · medium before anatomy

Ask for the body in the medium that draws it

Asked for a single-line stick torso in a soft cartoon hand, the model drew a pear — twice, past a negative naming filled body, wide body and thick torso. Asked for the same figure in plain ink it drew a true stick figure immediately. Choose the medium that makes the shape natural, then let colour live in the scene instead of the character.

R8 · generate small, deliver large

Generation size and delivery size are different decisions

The same still took four and a half minutes at 1080×1920 and about one minute at 576×1024. Generate at the smaller canvas the graphs were measured at; scale up when the film is assembled.

The open problem

The narration is the weak half of every episode

The pictures are ready to be a house style. The audio is not, and the reason is structural rather than a bad voice setting.

Each caption is synthesised on its own and dropped into a slot the picture already decided. Nothing carries across a sentence boundary: no phrasing over a full thought, no breath before a turn, no sentence that is allowed to run half a second long because it means something. The hosted narrator was also measured reading between 52 and 179 words per minute for the same line on different attempts, so the slot and the reading are never quite the same length. What comes out is legible and mechanical.

Two smaller faults sit beside it: the render provenance records nothing about which narrator spoke or what it cost, so the only way to tell a paid read from the free one afterwards was the file size; and the caption track is generated from the same per-shot lines, so it inherits the same chopping.

In order of how much they would fix

  • Cut the picture to the audio, not the audio to the picture. Write the whole script, have it read as one continuous take, then set each shot's duration from where its sentence actually falls. This is how the reference channels are made and it removes the mechanical pacing at the root. It needs one new step in the pipeline: measure the narration, then write the shot durations from it.
  • Try a voice built for the language. The current hosted voice takes a direction and reads Thai adequately, not well. Worth comparing against a provider whose Thai is a first-class voice rather than a multilingual fallback — and worth trying English narration, where the same provider is noticeably stronger.
  • Record a human read for the pilot. Five minutes of script is twenty minutes of recording. For deciding whether the format works at all, a real voice removes the one variable that is currently hiding the answer.
  • Record the voice in the provenance. Provider, voice, model, characters and cost belong in the render sidecar beside the picture's lineage. Small, and it makes the comparison above measurable.

Burned-in subtitles are optional and currently carry the same chopping as the audio. Until the pacing is fixed, an episode reads better with clean pictures and no burned captions than with both.

What has not been tried

  • A LoRA trained on the approved character. Worth considering only after a curated pose library exists and the edit graph still cannot hold something specific.
  • Cutaway inserts of props with no character, which R3 makes cheap and which would break up long stretches of one figure on one ground.
  • Music. Every episode so far is narration and silence.
Measured on one workstation: RTX 5080, ComfyUI 0.34.0, Z-Image Turbo and Qwen-Image-Edit 2511 with the Lightning 4-step LoRA. Timings will move on other hardware; the rules will not.
